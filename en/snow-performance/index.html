Real physical demands

SKIING IS MORE DEMANDING THAN IT SEEMS.

Skiing and snowboarding demand far more from your body than you might realize.

Your body must generate force, maintain balance, control motion, and react quickly for hours on end. As the day wears on, fatigue sets in and sustaining optimal movement quality becomes increasingly difficult.

01 · Morning

First run

Neuromuscular freshness, optimal reflexes, and sharp responsiveness to terrain irregularities.

02 · Midday

More runs

Cumulative load on quadriceps, glutes, and core. Sustaining proper posture begins requiring greater effort.

03 · Afternoon

Fatigue

Decreased reactivity. Snow turns heavier, and edge-to-edge transitions demand significantly more energy.

04 · Final run

Reduced control

Emergence of mechanical compensations and loss of precision in tight turns and unexpected terrain.

Determining capacities

The five dimensions of snow performance

Complete physical conditioning is not just about lifting weights: it optimizes how your entire system interacts in rapidly shifting conditions.

Strength

Structural strength

Build the strength you need on the slopes.

We develop eccentric strength in quads, glutes, and hamstrings to absorb turn compressions efficiently and hold an athletic stance without collapsing.

Balance

Dynamic balance

Improve stability and control.

Single-leg stability and unsteady surface training simulating sudden transitions from powder to hardpack or unexpected moguls.

Control

Motor precision

Move with more confidence.

We train trunk rotational control and lumbo-pelvic dissociation so your turning technique remains intact as the hours tick by.

Power

Explosive reactivity

Be ready for quick, dynamic movements.

Plyometric work and rate of force development (RFD) to react in time to bumps, natural drops, and sudden gradient changes.

Endurance

Neuromuscular stamina

Ski and ride for longer with less fatigue.

Specific conditioning to extend your work capacity and enjoy full mountain days with sustained energy and pleasure.
